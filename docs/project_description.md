Final Project

In the final project, you as a team will (a) explore dataset(s) that is either accessible to the public, crawled by your own team or collected from other resources and make sure that the data can be used by your team for scientific purpose; (b) propose the corresponding problems that are interesting (e.g. from the perspective of data interpretation or methodology) and you can solve under the general theme: what can we learn from the data; (c) Solve the problems using methods from this course (network characterization and data driven modeling) and/or novel methods that you would like to develop (related to this course); (d) Explain and interpret your results. 

It is advisable to apply methods from both network characterisation and data driven modelling (e.g., influence of network topology on process, relation between topological properties of a node and role of the node in a process, process modelling and process interventions) to ensure the magnitude/breath of your project. 

Evaluation is based on the quality of your project reflected from the final presentation and the question&answer session after your presentation. The quality of a project refers to e.g. the rigorousness (scientific soundness), magnitude/breath and novelty/originality/scientific depth of the project.

You are required to make slides to include key results to present. Your slides could include e.g. description of your dataset, the objective of your project, methods, results, explanation and interpretations (message, impact) of your results, and a conclusion.

Two Meetings will be planned for each group together with the teacher. 

Feedback meeting <=40min (~12/10-16/10): 5-10min presentation of your dataset(s), problems, motivation, approaches, which will be discussed further.
Focus: problem definition, feedback on initial result

Final evaluation meeting <=35min (~26/10-2/11): 15min presentation (sharp! have to stop after 15min. Only what you have presented will be evaluated. At least 3 students talk during the presentation) and a 10-15min Question and Answer session. Normally, the evaluation/grades will be given by the end of the interview. Please submit your slides via brightspace Assignments/Final project before your final interview (your first slide should include Final Project Group number, title of your project, name and student number of each team member). Please save your dataset and programming code used for data analyse at least until week 2.4, which can be requested after your presentation. No need to submit them if not requested. 

Important: If you are going to use public available datasets, please refer to the dataset correctly (e.g. to the website or a paper as required by the owner of the dataset) in your slides.

Examples of open datasets:

http://snap.stanford.edu/data/

http://konect.uni-koblenz.de

Many temporal social contact networks (in the form of  pairwise or higher-order temporal networks) are publicly available, see papers that use these datasets: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03020v1, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0323753

Note that some datasets contain only static network topology without status/activities of nodes/links. This can be limited to explore process (information diffusion, social contagion) related problems.
